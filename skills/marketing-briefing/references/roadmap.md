# Marketing Decision Roadmap (12 phases)

Each phase lists the decisions it must produce. The consultant's job is to draw
these decisions out of the founder, one question at a time, validating against
the cited frameworks. This file is the source for `tmp/marketing-roadmap.md`.

The sequence matters: each phase feeds the next. Positioning errors waste ad
spend; pricing before PMF scales a product that doesn't convert; growth before
retention is a leaky bucket. Phases 0-7 are strategy (months-years horizon);
phases 8-12 are execution (weeks-months).

---

## Phase 0 — Founder bedrock

Before any framework applies, the founder needs an articulated foundation.

Decisions:
1. Founding insight / "Why now?" — what does the founder know that others
   don't, and why does the opportunity exist now? (Play Bigger's POV)
2. Contrarian worldview — the non-obvious belief that organizes everything.
   Without it there is no real differentiation, only features.
3. Transformation hypothesis — how the world looks different if they win.

Output: a 1-2 page POV document. The north star for everything else.

## Phase 1 — Market reality

Research phase. Decisions here are methodological but critical.

Decisions:
1. ICP hypothesis vs. discovered ICP — who they THINK the ideal customer is,
   and the process to validate/refute it.
2. JTBD (Christensen) — what jobs (functional, social, emotional) is the
   customer hiring the product to do?
3. Competitive alternatives map (Dunford) — not just direct competitors:
   "do nothing", spreadsheet, hiring a human, adjacent tools. What does the
   customer compare against?
4. Customer research plan — how many interviews, what questions (focus on the
   problem, not the product).
5. Buying process map — who identifies the problem, decides, approves, uses,
   pays. Critical in B2B SaaS.

## Phase 2 — Positioning (Dunford, 5 components)

The highest-leverage strategic decision. Without it, all spend is wasted.

Decisions:
1. Competitive alternatives — exhaustive list, including the status quo.
2. Unique attributes — capabilities NO alternative has (capabilities, not
   features).
3. Value delivered — what benefit each unique attribute enables, in customer
   language.
4. Best-fit customers — who cares MOST about that value; demographic +
   situational + psychographic markers that predict urgency.
5. Market category — the context in which they want to be evaluated. This sets
   competitors, table stakes, and the price reference the customer brings.

Wrong-positioning symptoms (trigger to revisit): long sales cycle, prospects
who "don't get it", churn from bad fit.

## Phase 3 — Category strategy (Play Bigger)

Positioning says where to play within a category. Category says what the game
is.

Decisions:
1. Category fit — play in (a) existing category, (b) new sub-category, or
   (c) entirely new category? New category captures ~76% of category economics
   but demands heavy education investment.
2. Name the problem — what problem the category solves. Naming an unnamed
   problem is often more powerful than naming the solution.
3. Category name — short, distinct, memorable.
4. Lightning strike — when/how to claim the category; a concentrated launch
   that signals who the king is.
5. Category manifesto — public document: "the world was X, it changed to Y,
   therefore this category must exist."

## Phase 4 — Moats (7 Powers, Helmer)

Cheap marketing is easy; durable marketing needs a moat.

Decisions:
1. Which of the 7 powers are being built? Scale, network, counter-positioning,
   switching costs, branding, cornered resource, process power.
2. Branding as moat — willing to invest the years required? What price premium
   to sustain?
3. Network economies — does the product improve with more users? How does
   marketing accelerate the loop?
4. Switching costs — what lock-in (data, integrations, habits, internal
   networks) is built deliberately?
5. Counter-positioning — a model incumbents can't copy without cannibalizing?
   Marketing should emphasize it.

Each power = Benefit + Barrier. If "how to copy" is obvious, it's not a power.

## Phase 5 — Brand narrative & identity (StoryBrand SB7)

Strategy (0-4) is the truths. Narrative is how they're told.

Decisions:
1. Full BrandScript (SB7): character (customer) and what they want; problem
   (external, internal, philosophical); brand as guide (empathy + authority);
   the plan; direct + transitional CTA; what failure is avoided (stakes); the
   transformation (aspirational identity).
2. One-liner — "We help [X] who struggle with [Y] do [Z] so they can [W]."
3. Brand archetype — Sage, Hero, Caregiver, Magician, etc.
4. Voice & tone — vocabulary, rhythm, triggers to avoid; documented.
5. Visual identity — logo, type, palette, system; reflects positioning +
   archetype.
6. Naming (if undecided) — pronounceable, domain free, no bad connotations,
   evokes the category.

## Phase 6 — Pricing & packaging (Monetizing Innovation)

Pricing is business architecture, not a final decision. Ramanujam's 9 rules.

Decisions:
1. WTP research — willingness-to-pay conversations with customers BEFORE
   building pricing; which method (van Westendorp, conjoint, Gabor-Granger).
2. Segment by WTP, not demographics — different customers pay different amounts
   for the SAME product; how to separate them.
3. Pricing model — subscription, per-seat, usage-based, freemium, tiered,
   value-based; each has strategic implications.
4. Tier architecture — how many, what goes in each; good-better-best is the
   norm; >4 confuses.
5. Anchor & decoy — which tier anchors (most visible, priciest), which is the
   decoy.
6. Free vs trial vs freemium vs demo — central choice; each affects CAC,
   activation, lead quality, sales motion.
7. Pricing strategy — skim, penetration, neutral; coherent with positioning.
8. Avoid the 4 failures — feature shock, minivation, hidden gem, undead.
9. Value story — every R$ charged justified in value delivered.

## Phase 7 — PMF gate (Hacking Growth, precondition)

Do not invest in growth before PMF. Scaling a product without PMF burns money
and bakes in churn.

Decisions:
1. Sean Ellis test — "how disappointed if you could no longer use this?" 40%+
   "very disappointed" signals PMF.
2. North Star Metric — one metric representing delivered value (not revenue,
   not MAU).
3. Aha moment / activation — the action that predicts retention.
4. Retention curve — flattens (PMF) or decays to zero (no PMF)?
5. Explicit "go to growth" criterion — pre-agreed: until X, don't scale
   acquisition.

## Phase 8 — Channel strategy (Traction, Bullseye)

Before spending on any channel, run the Bullseye.

Decisions:
1. Brainstorm all 19 channels — imagine each working spectacularly (anti-bias:
   force consideration of all).
2. Ring classification — inner (3 likely), middle (possible), outer
   (discarded).
3. Inner-ring test plan — hypothesis, budget, timeframe, success criterion for
   each, before starting.
4. Per-channel metrics — CAC, LTV/CAC, payback, lead quality (not just volume).
5. Critical path — shortest path to the next milestone (e.g. R$X MRR); every
   channel decision subordinate to it.
6. Post-validation focus — validated one channel? Double down before testing
   others. The #1 error is diversifying too early.

## Phase 9 — Content & trust engine (They Ask You Answer)

Applies if SEO or content are in the inner ring; otherwise skip.

Decisions:
1. Big 5 commitment — publish honestly about price, problems, comparisons,
   "best of" lists, reviews?
2. Insourcing vs outsourcing — internal expert (Sheridan recommends) or agency?
   Affects authenticity and depth.
3. Assignment selling — sales sends content pre-call? How to operationalize?
4. Editorial cadence — volume vs depth; calendar.
5. SEO architecture — pillar pages, topic clusters, search intent, keyword
   strategy.
6. Video — Sheridan insists it's non-negotiable; which formats?
7. Distribution — SEO, newsletter, social, podcast, partnerships; how each
   piece is amplified.

## Phase 10 — Conversion architecture (StoryBrand applied + Cialdini)

Traffic without conversion is vanity.

Decisions:
1. Full funnel — awareness → interest → consideration → decision → action →
   retention → advocacy; what offer serves each stage.
2. Landing pages with SB7 — header (one-liner), value prop, stakes, plan,
   transitional CTA, direct CTA; every LP follows it.
3. Direct vs transitional CTAs — 95% of visitors aren't ready to buy.
4. Apply Cialdini's 7 principles (where, when, which touchpoint): reciprocity,
   commitment/consistency, social proof, authority, liking, scarcity, unity.
5. Friction audit — where users drop; form fields, social login, card required
   on trial.
6. A/B testing roadmap — hypotheses prioritized by ICE (impact, confidence,
   ease).

## Phase 11 — Growth loops & virality (Hacking Growth + Contagious)

Paid channels are spend; loops are compound interest.

Decisions:
1. AARRR by stage — acquisition, activation, retention, referral, revenue;
   metric and target for each.
2. Loop identification — where in the funnel one customer creates more
   customers (referral, content, network, integration, marketplace).
3. Experimentation cadence — weekly growth sprint? How many tests/week? Who
   prioritizes?
4. Team structure — PM, eng, marketing, data (even partial).
5. STEPPS analysis (Berger) for product/content — social currency, triggers,
   emotion, public, practical value, stories.
6. Formal referral program — incentive, mechanic, comms; when to activate.
7. Loops vs channels — channels scale linearly with spend, loops non-linearly;
   where each marginal R$ goes.

## Phase 12 — Retention, expansion, measurement

Growing and losing is double the work.

Decisions:
1. Churn definition — cancellation, inactivity, downgrade; each has a different
   playbook.
2. Onboarding — steps to aha moment; time to first win; email/in-app
   touchpoints.
3. Expansion playbook — upsell, cross-sell, seat expansion; automated or human.
4. NRR target — above 100% is a compounding engine.
5. Customer Success vs Marketing boundary — where each starts; who owns
   lifecycle marketing.
6. Community — build owned community (Slack/Discord/forum)? High cost, high
   moat.
7. Funnel KPIs by stage — awareness (reach, SOV), consideration (CTR, MQL),
   decision (SQL, demos, trial start), action (conversion, CAC, payback),
   retention (NRR, NPS, churn).
8. Attribution model — first-touch, last-touch, multi-touch, MMM; decide before
   spending.
9. Operating cadence — daily standup, weekly review, monthly board, quarterly
   OKRs; who attends.
10. Anti-vanity — which metrics to commit to ignoring (followers, impressions,
    ungated downloads).

---

## End state

When the briefing is complete, the answers log is a full marketing brief. The
most valuable artifacts: POV (Phase 0) + positioning statement (Phase 2) +
category manifesto (Phase 3) + BrandScript (Phase 5) + pricing story (Phase 6).
Five pages that govern every future decision.
